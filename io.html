Chapter 6 • Input and Output

Input & Output Devices

Computers use input and output devices to communicate with people. Here are eight common examples, what they look like, and what each one does.

The difference: Input devices send information into a computer. Output devices present information from a computer.
Explore Input Devices ↓Explore Output Devices ↓
01 / GIVING INFORMATION

Input Devices

Input devices let people send information and instructions to a computer.

INPUT DEVICE 01

Keyboard

A keyboard is an input device used to type letters, numbers, and commands into a computer. It is helpful for writing documents, searching the internet, and entering information.

INPUT DEVICE 02

Mouse

A mouse controls the pointer on a screen. Moving, clicking, and scrolling allow a user to select buttons, open programs, and move through web pages.

INPUT DEVICE 03

Scanner

A scanner takes a picture of a paper document or photograph and turns it into a digital file. It is often used to save or share printed information.

INPUT DEVICE 04

Microphone

A microphone captures sound and sends it to a computer or phone as audio input. People use it for video calls, voice recordings, and speech recognition.

02 / RECEIVING INFORMATION

Output Devices

Output devices let a computer communicate results back to people.

OUTPUT DEVICE 05

Monitor

A monitor displays visual information from the computer, including text, photos, videos, and applications. It lets users see the results of their actions.

OUTPUT DEVICE 06

Printer

A printer produces a physical copy of digital documents or images on paper. It can be used to print school assignments, forms, and photographs.

OUTPUT DEVICE 07

Speakers

Speakers turn digital audio signals into sounds that people can hear. They are used for music, videos, notifications, and online meetings.

OUTPUT DEVICE 08

Projector

A projector displays a computer’s images or videos on a larger surface, such as a screen or wall. It is useful for classroom lessons and presentations.

Quick Review

Input: A keyboard, mouse, scanner, and microphone help people send data to a computer.

Output: A monitor, printer, speakers, and projector help people see, hear, or print information from a computer.

Some devices, such as a touchscreen, can act as both input and output devices.
