Chapter 5 • Digital Security and Ethics

Staying Safe and Responsible Online

Technology is part of everyday life. Knowing how to protect your accounts, recognize scams, and use AI responsibly makes the internet safer for everyone.

This page covers six important topics: strong passwords, phishing attacks, malware, online privacy, AI ethics, and a safe password-strength demonstration.
01 / ACCOUNT SECURITY

Strong Passwords

A strong password is hard for people or computer programs to guess. Longer, unpredictable passwords are usually more secure than short passwords, especially if they avoid names, birthdays, and familiar phrases.

  • When making your own password, aim for at least 14–16 characters, or use an even longer password when allowed.
  • Letters, numbers, and symbols can add variety, but length and unpredictability matter most.
  • Use a different password on every account. If one site is hacked, reused passwords can expose other accounts.
  • A password manager helps create and save random, unique passwords.
  • Multifactor authentication (MFA) adds extra protection through an authenticator app, security key, or another sign-in method.

Important: Never share your passwords with anyone, and never reuse an easy-to-guess password.

02 / SPOT THE SCAM

Phishing Attacks

Phishing is when scammers pretend to be a trusted person or organization to trick someone into revealing a password, financial detail, or other private information. Phishing can happen through emails, texts, social media, and fake websites.

Made-up suspicious email: “URGENT: Your school account closes in 10 minutes! Click account-check.example and enter your password!”
Made-up suspicious text: “Your package cannot be delivered. Click this link and pay a small fee.”

Warning signs: unexpected links, strange sender addresses, pressure to act immediately, surprise payment requests, and demands for login details.

Stay safe: Check the full website address and sender, avoid unexpected attachments, and go to the organization's official app or known website instead of following a suspicious link. If in doubt, contact the organization through a trusted channel.

The “.example” domain above is a harmless placeholder, not a real login website.

03 / PROTECT DEVICES

Malware

Malware is harmful software designed to disrupt a device, steal data, or prevent you from using files.

  • Viruses: Harmful code that attaches to files or programs and can spread when those files run.
  • Ransomware: Malware that blocks access to files and demands payment.
  • Spyware: Software that secretly monitors activity or collects information.

Computers and phones can become infected through unsafe downloads, fake apps, malicious email attachments, compromised sites, and outdated software.

Prevention: Update your system and apps, download from trusted sources, avoid suspicious attachments, keep security protections turned on, and back up important files.

04 / PERSONAL INFORMATION

Online Privacy

Online privacy means controlling who can see and use your personal information. Sharing too much can expose your location, phone number, school, accounts, or other sensitive details.

  • Review privacy settings on social media and decide who can view your posts and location.
  • Websites and apps collect data about browsing and interests. Review permissions and turn off unnecessary tracking.
  • Use HTTPS websites, but remember that HTTPS alone does not prove a site is honest.
  • Do not publicly post passwords, identification numbers, home addresses, or financial details.
  • Think before posting because screenshots and saved copies can remain after a post is deleted.
05 / RESPONSIBLE TECHNOLOGY

AI Ethics

Artificial intelligence can help people learn, write, research, and solve problems, but it must be used responsibly.

  • Bias: AI can produce unfair outcomes if the information used to train it contains unfair patterns.
  • Privacy: Entering private information into an AI tool may expose details that should be kept confidential.
  • Misinformation: AI may produce convincing answers, images, or audio that are false or misleading.
  • Responsible use: Use AI to support learning, not to deceive people or present unverified claims as facts.
  • Verification: Double-check important AI-generated statements against trustworthy sources because AI sometimes makes mistakes.
  • Intellectual property: Respect creators' work. Do not copy protected material without permission, and credit sources when needed.
QUICK REMINDER

Smart Digital Habits

Staying safe online means paying attention before you click, share, download, or sign in. Small habits can prevent many common digital problems.

  • Stop and think before clicking unexpected links.
  • Use unique, strong passwords and enable MFA.
  • Keep devices and apps updated.
  • Share less personal information publicly.
  • Verify AI-generated information before trusting or sharing it.
